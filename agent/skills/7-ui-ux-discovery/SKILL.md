# 7-ui-ux-discovery — UX Review and Product Feel

Use this skill before any redesign or page implementation work. It exists to slow the process down enough to make the UI feel human, calm, and believable.

## Goal

Review the current application experience and decide what feels off in tone, structure, flow, trust, and clarity. Turn that into a short, clear UX direction so later UI work is deliberate instead of decorative.

## What to check

Look at the current app from the user's point of view:

- Does the app feel personal or generic?
- Does the wording sound human, warm, and believable?
- Is the flow easy to understand without explanation?
- Are the screens visually calm or visually noisy?
- Are there any sections that feel like a marketing page instead of a lived-in product?
- Does the app feel emotionally supportive or overly clever?

## Questions to answer

1. What is the overall feeling the app should create?
2. What tone does the product currently have?
3. Which screens feel like they need a more human voice?
4. Which parts feel cluttered, spammy, or template-like?
5. What is the simplest, clearest path for a user from first screen to useful outcome?
6. What should the app feel like in the user's mind after 10 seconds?

## Decision rules

- Prefer natural language over polished jargon.
- Prefer calm structure over visual noise.
- Prefer emotional clarity over cleverness.
- Prefer a small, believable product over a busy feature list.
- If something feels like an AI demo or marketing template, remove or simplify it.
- Keep the product grounded in real human behavior, not generic SaaS language.

## Output expected

Write a short UX review in plain language with these sections:

### 1. Current feeling
Describe the app's current mood and whether it is helping or hurting the product.

### 2. What feels off
List the UI or copy issues that reduce trust or make the experience feel fake, noisy, or generic.

### 3. UX direction
State the desired tone and behavior in 3–5 guiding principles.

### 4. Recommended next improvements
List the specific screens or sections to refine first, in priority order.

## Important

Do not start implementing visual changes until the UX direction is clear and approved. This step is about making the product feel genuinely human before the interface is refined.
